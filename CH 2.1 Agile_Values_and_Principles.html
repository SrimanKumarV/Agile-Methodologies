1 Before the Values

What Is Agile, Really?

Before the four values and twelve principles make sense individually, it helps to know what "Agile" is actually naming.

SPEC-00AGILE
Definition
A set of methods and methodologies that help a team think more effectively, work more efficiently, and make better decisions — covering project management, software design, and process improvement.
Also a Mindset
The right mindset is what makes any of these practices actually work well.
Common Methodologies
Scrum · Kanban · Extreme Programming (XP) · Lean.
Origin
The Agile Manifesto was written in 2001 by 17 software experts, opening with: "We are uncovering better ways of developing software by doing it and helping others do it." It introduced four values and twelve principles.

3 Value 1

Individuals & Interactions — over — Processes & Tools

Individuals & InteractionsoverProcesses & Tools

People are more important than processes and tools. Processes and software are useful, but they can't replace real communication, teamwork, and collaboration. A skilled team with simple tools can succeed; a team with the best tools but poor communication is likely to fail.

People build software — not tools.
Team A — Fails
  • Expensive software
  • Latest laptops
  • Many management tools
  • Never communicates
Team B — Succeeds
  • Ordinary laptops
  • Free software
  • Meets every day
  • Discusses problems, helps each other
FoodExpress Moment

A tester finds a bug in FoodExpress. Instead of two days of back-and-forth email, they simply walk over and say "The Login button isn't working." The developer fixes it in five minutes. Quick, direct communication beat any tool.

4 Value 2

Working Software — over — Comprehensive Documentation

Working SoftwareoverComprehensive Documentation

Documentation is necessary, but the main goal of software development is working software. A project with hundreds of pages of documents and no working application has little value to the customer — Agile teams document only what's needed while continuously delivering something that runs.

Customers pay for software — not documents.
The Restaurant Analogy

Imagine a waiter hands you a menu, a recipe book, and cooking instructions — but no food. Are you satisfied? No — you came for the food. In the same way, a customer who receives 500 pages of specifications but no working FoodExpress app isn't satisfied either.

5 Value 3

Customer Collaboration — over — Contract Negotiation

Customer CollaborationoverContract Negotiation

Traditional projects focus heavily on the signed contract, with little interaction afterward. Agile instead keeps the conversation going continuously between customer and developers — feedback along the way ensures the software actually fits real needs, not just what was written down at the start.

Work with the customer, not just the contract.
The Birthday Cake

You order a birthday cake. Midway through, the baker asks: "Would you like chocolate decoration?" You say yes — and the design updates. Without that small collaboration, the cake might not match what you actually wanted.

FoodExpress: Week 1, Mr. Kumar asks for a login system. Week 2, he asks for Google Login too. The team discusses it and adds it — customer satisfaction goes up, not down.

6 Value 4

Responding to Change — over — Following a Plan

Responding to ChangeoverFollowing a Plan

There's an old saying: "plan the work, work the plan." But if you work the wrong plan, you build the wrong product. Requirements change because of new business needs, feedback, competition, or new technology — Agile teams watch for that change and adapt, rather than rigidly sticking to an outdated plan.

Adapt to change instead of resisting it.
The Rained-Out College Tour

A college tour is planned outdoors. Heavy rain suddenly begins. Do you stick to the original plan anyway? No — you move everything indoors.

FoodExpress: the app initially supports only Cash on Delivery. When customers start asking for UPI, the Agile team accepts the change, adds the feature, and releases an update — instead of saying "requirement closed."

7 Putting the Values Into Practice

The 12 Agile Principles

If the four values are the "heart" of Agile, the twelve principles are how that heart actually beats, day to day. Every one of them plays out inside the same FoodExpress project — tap each one open.

1Customer Satisfaction Through Early & Continuous Delivery😊 Satisfaction
"Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software."

Rather than waiting until the whole project is finished, Agile teams split the work into small, usable increments — each one delivers real value and gathers feedback for what comes next.

FoodExpress: instead of promising everything after one year, the team delivers Login and Food Ordering within two weeks. Mr. Kumar starts earning money almost immediately, while Payment, Live Tracking, and Coupons follow in later sprints.

Memory: Deliver → Feedback → Improve → Happy Customer
2Welcome Changing Requirements🔄 Change
"Welcome changing requirements, even late in development. Agile processes harness change for the customer's competitive advantage."

Customer needs, business conditions, and technology keep evolving — Agile treats change as an opportunity for better software, not a problem to reject.

FoodExpress: three months in, Mr. Kumar says "everyone pays with UPI now." A traditional team would say "requirements frozen." The Agile team instead says "excellent idea" and adds it next sprint — the restaurant gains more customers.

Memory: Change → Improve → Compete
3Deliver Working Software Frequently🚀 Frequency
"Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to the shorter timescale."

Regular, short releases mean customers keep seeing improvements instead of waiting for one giant release at the very end.

FoodExpress: Sprint 1 delivers Ordering, Sprint 2 Payment, Sprint 3 Tracking, Sprint 4 Reviews — every month brings a visible improvement, similar to how Chrome updates regularly instead of once every five years.

Memory: Small → Fast → Frequent → Better
4Business People & Developers Work Together Daily🤝 Collaboration
"Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project."

Daily communication clarifies requirements, resolves issues quickly, and keeps the product aligned with what the business actually needs.

FoodExpress: Mr. Kumar asks for "today's menu," but the developer builds a weekly menu instead — a misunderstanding. Once daily discussions start, that gap disappears entirely.

Memory: Talk → Understand → Build Correctly
5Build Projects Around Motivated Individuals💪 Motivation
"Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done."

Instead of controlling every small task, Agile trusts that motivated people — given the right environment and support — naturally produce better software.

FoodExpress: Manager Ravi used to check every developer every 10 minutes; progress was slow. After learning to say "I trust you, tell me if you need help," the team completes Payment, Coupons, and Notifications faster than ever.

Memory: Trust → Motivation → Success
6Face-to-Face Conversation Is Most Effective💬 Communication
"The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a development team is face-to-face conversation."

Direct conversation clears up confusion far faster than long email chains — though video calls count too, when meeting in person isn't possible.

FoodExpress: weeks were wasted exchanging emails about festival offers. A single ten-minute sit-down between customer and developer cleared up the requirement instantly.

Memory: Talk → Understand → Build
7Working Software Is the Primary Measure of Progress💻 Progress
"Working software is the primary measure of progress."

Reports, meetings, and page counts don't prove progress — only software that actually runs and delivers value does.

FoodExpress: Team A reports "300 pages of documentation written." Team B reports "500 food orders already placed by customers." Team B is the one that actually made progress.

Memory: Software → Customer → Progress
8Agile Promotes Sustainable Development⚖️ Sustainability
"Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely."

A steady, healthy pace beats short bursts of overtime — like a marathon runner pacing themselves instead of sprinting the first mile and collapsing later.

FoodExpress: forcing ten new features in one week exhausts the team and increases bugs. The Scrum Master instead spreads the work across multiple sprints — quality goes back up.

Memory: Steady Pace → Healthy Team → Quality Software
9Technical Excellence & Good Design Enhance Agility🏗️ Excellence
"Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility."

Clean, well-designed code is easier to modify, test, and extend — exactly like a well-built house lasts for years, while a cheaply built one starts cracking within one.

FoodExpress: messy code means one new feature breaks five others. After the team schedules time to refactor and clean up the architecture, adding Loyalty Points, Gift Cards, and a Referral System becomes easy.

Memory: Good Design → Easy Change → Agility
10Simplicity — Maximizing Work Not Done✂️ Simplicity
"Simplicity — the art of maximizing the amount of work not done — is essential."

Build only what's actually needed now, not everything that might possibly be useful someday — extra, unrequested work wastes both time and money.

FoodExpress: Mr. Kumar asks only for Login. A developer who also adds an AI chatbot, VR menu, and blockchain payments wastes months on things nobody asked for. The Agile team builds exactly Login, Ordering, and Payment — nothing more.

Memory: Need → Build → Deliver
11Best Designs Emerge From Self-Organizing Teams👥 Self-Organizing
"The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams."

Teams perform better when trusted to make their own technical decisions, instead of being directed at every single step by a manager who may not know the technical details best.

FoodExpress: a manager insists on "Database A," ignoring the team's suggestion of a faster option. Once he lets the team decide, they choose the better database and performance doubles.

Memory: Trust Team → Team Thinks → Best Solution
12Regular Reflection to Become More Effective📈 Improvement
"At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly."

Without reflection, the same mistakes repeat sprint after sprint — Agile teams instead pause regularly to ask what worked, what didn't, and what to change next.

FoodExpress: after one sprint, the team notices testing started too late, causing bugs. Next sprint, testing starts from day one — and bugs drop noticeably.

Memory: Review → Learn → Improve → Repeat
"Working software over documents. People over tools. Customers as partners, not signatures on a contract. And change welcomed, not feared — that's the whole spirit of Agile, in one sentence."

✓ The Biggest Lesson

Agile Is a Mindset

Agile focuses on delivering customer value through collaboration, adaptability, quality, simplicity, empowered teams, and continuous improvement. Scrum is one practical framework that turns these values and principles into everyday practice — through Sprints, Reviews, and Retrospectives.

Glossary

TermMeaning
AgileA mindset and set of methodologies for working effectively and adapting to change
Agile ManifestoA 2001 document by 17 software experts introducing 4 values and 12 principles
Agile ValueA core belief about what to prioritize — stated as "X over Y"
Agile PrincipleA guideline that puts the Agile values into everyday practice
IncrementA small, usable piece of software delivered during development
SprintA short, fixed time period in which a team delivers a working increment
Sustainable PaceA steady work rhythm the team can maintain indefinitely without burnout
Self-Organizing TeamA team trusted to make its own technical and organizational decisions
Sprint RetrospectiveA regular meeting where the team reflects and improves its way of working
ScrumA practical framework that implements Agile values and principles through Sprints, Reviews, and Retrospectives
