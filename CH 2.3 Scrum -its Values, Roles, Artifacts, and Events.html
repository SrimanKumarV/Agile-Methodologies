The Scenario

Five second-year students — a developer, a designer, a tester, a database person, and one more all-rounder — decide to build a College Food Delivery App so students can order from campus canteens without standing in line. Nobody is "the boss." There's no project manager assigning tasks every morning. The team has to organize itself.

That single constraint — no manager micromanaging every task — is exactly the problem Scrum was designed to solve. Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps small, self-organizing teams build a product in short, repeatable cycles, while staying honest about progress at every step.

Ask yourself first

If you were on this team with no one assigning your day, what qualities would you need to actually get things done without chaos? Keep that question in mind — the answer is the next section.

Scrum Values — how the team behaves

Scrum Value: a principle that guides how Scrum Team members behave and work together, without needing someone to enforce it. There are five of them. Tap any card to see the full explanation and how it plays out in the Food Delivery App team.

01 / 05

🤝 Commitment

Each team member is dedicated to achieving the goals of the project and the Sprint.

tap to expand ↓
In plain words

It's not "I finished my task, so I don't care about the rest." It's "we agreed on a goal together, and I'm responsible for helping the whole team reach it."

In the food delivery app

The team agrees: "By the end of this Sprint, students should be able to log in and view the menu." The developer builds Login, the designer builds the UI, the tester tests it — but everyone stays invested until the whole goal is met, not just their own slice.

⭐ Remember: Commitment = dedication toward the project goal.
02 / 05

🎯 Focus

Concentrating on the work and goals of the current Sprint, instead of chasing unrelated ideas.

tap to expand ↓
In plain words

New ideas will always show up mid-project. Focus means finishing what you committed to before chasing the next shiny thing.

In the food delivery app

The current Sprint is Login + Menu. Midway through, someone gets excited: "Let's also add an AI chatbot, coupons, dark mode, and voice ordering!" A focused team says: great ideas — for a future Sprint. Right now, we finish Login + Menu.

⭐ Remember: Focus = concentrate on the current goal, not every good idea that shows up.
03 / 05

👁️ Openness

Team members openly share their progress, problems, and the real state of the project — nothing is hidden.

tap to expand ↓
In plain words

Everyone should know what everyone else is working on, what's blocked, and what's done — no surprises at the last moment.

In the food delivery app

The developer hits a wall: "The payment API is failing." Hiding it helps no one. Saying it out loud — "I'm blocked by the payment API" — lets the team respond. A shared task board (To Do / In Progress / Done) makes this visible to everyone at a glance.

⭐ Remember: Openness = make your work and your problems visible.
04 / 05

🙌 Respect

Team members value one another's skills, contributions, responsibilities, and opinions.

tap to expand ↓
In plain words

Mutual respect is what lets people trust each other to do their part well — even when they work very differently.

In the food delivery app

The tester reports: "There's a serious bug in Login." A disrespectful developer says "you don't understand coding." A respectful one says: "let's investigate this together." Both are working toward the same goal — the bug isn't a personal attack.

⭐ Remember: Respect = trust and value your teammates' work.
05 / 05

🦁 Courage

The willingness to stand up for the project, raise hard truths, and follow Agile principles even under pressure.

tap to expand ↓
In plain words

Courage is what makes the other four values real when it's uncomfortable to be honest.

In the food delivery app

A manager pressures the team: "The feature is only 50% done — demo it as finished." A courageous team says: "we can't present unfinished work as complete." Or a developer realizes a requirement is technically impossible in this Sprint — courage means saying so early, not staying quiet.

⭐ Remember: Courage = speak up and do what's right for the project.

Part 2 of 4

Scrum Roles — who owns what

Values tell us how the team behaves. Roles tell us who is responsible for what. Scrum keeps this deliberately small — just three roles.

👑

Product Owner

Responsible for the Product Backlog — its contents, its priority, and the key product decisions. Answers the question "what should we build first?"

Food delivery app example
The backlog has Login, Menu, Search, Cart, Payment, Tracking, and Feedback. The Product Owner decides: "Login and Menu matter most right now" — so those get top priority.
Product Owner → WHAT + PRIORITY
🛠️

Development Team

The cross-functional group of technical members — developers, testers, designers, and specialists — who work together to actually build the product.

Food delivery app example
Developer writes code, Tester checks quality, UI/UX Designer shapes the screens, Database Specialist manages storage — together they have every skill needed to ship the product.
Development Team → BUILD the product
🧭

Scrum Master

Helps the team work effectively within Scrum and removes obstacles that block progress. Not a boss — a facilitator, coach, and impediment-remover.

Food delivery app example
Developer: "The testing server is down." Scrum Master: "I'll get that resolved so you're not blocked." That's the job — clearing the path, not assigning tasks.
Scrum Master → HELP the team work effectively
Part 3 of 4

Scrum Artifacts — where the work lives

Scrum Artifacts are the work products that give everyone transparency about the product and the team's progress. There are three, and they flow into one another.

📋 Product Backlog

An ordered list of all the work required for the product. It's the master to-do list — everything the app could ever need, ranked by priority.

Example
Login, Menu, Search, Cart, Payment, Tracking, Feedback — the complete wish list for the Food Delivery App, in priority order.

🗂️ Sprint Backlog

The work selected for the current Sprint, plus the plan for completing it. It's a small, focused slice pulled from the Product Backlog.

Example
From the full backlog, the team pulls out Login, Menu, and Search for this Sprint — and maps out how they'll get it done.

✅ Increment

The working product outcome produced during the Sprint — usable, tested, and demonstrable, not "almost done."

Example
After Sprint 1: Login works, Menu works, Search works. That combined, working functionality is the Increment.
Part 4 of 4

Scrum Events — the rhythm the team follows

Scrum Events (sometimes called Scrum Rules) are the recurring meetings that structure every Sprint. They repeat in the same order, Sprint after Sprint, forming a cycle.

🗓️ Sprint Planning

The event at the start of a Sprint where the team decides what work will be done and how. It has two parts: (1) which backlog items can we deliver? (2) how will we accomplish them? Output: the Sprint Backlog.

🏃 The Sprint

A fixed-length period during which the team builds the selected backlog items into working software. Common lengths: two weeks or one month — but always fixed ("time-boxed") in advance.

💬 Daily Scrum simulator — a 15-minute meeting, 3 questions

⏱ 15-min time-box

🎬 Sprint Review

The meeting where the team demonstrates working software to users and stakeholders and collects feedback. Rule: only completed items get demonstrated. Login ✅ gets shown. Payment ❌ (not finished) does not get pretended into a demo.

🔁 Sprint Retrospective

The meeting where the team reflects on how it worked, not what it built. Two questions: "What went well?" and "What can improve?" Example: "Testing started too late" → action: "start testing earlier next Sprint."

Review vs. Retrospective — the exam favorite

These two get mixed up constantly. Here's the difference in one table.

Sprint ReviewSprint Retrospective
Product-focusedProcess-focused
Demonstrates working softwareImproves the way the team works
Stakeholders are involvedScrum Team only, focused on improvement
Gets feedback on the productGets ideas to improve the process
"What did we build?""How can we work better?"
Golden memory trick

REVIEW → PRODUCT.   RETROSPECTIVE → PROCESS.

Putting it together

The whole story, one diagram

Four pieces, one framework. Here's how Values, Roles, Artifacts, and Events all connect inside a single Sprint of the Food Delivery App.

Recap the whole journey

The team starts with Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage as their working culture. The Product Owner decides priorities, the Development Team builds, and the Scrum Master clears blockers. Work flows from the Product Backlog → into a focused Sprint Backlog → into a working Increment. And the whole thing runs on a rhythm: Sprint Planning → Sprint → Daily Scrum → Sprint Review → Sprint Retrospective — then the next Sprint begins.
